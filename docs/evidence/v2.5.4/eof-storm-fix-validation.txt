This file is the empirical evidence that issue #10's wedge is fixed in
v2.5.4 final, NOT just band-aided. The root-cause fix is in
`src/channel.c::channel_try_read` — `read() == 0` is now treated as
EAGAIN (chardev peer disconnect) instead of EOF (fatal device hangup).
See CHANGELOG and `docs/evidence/v2.5.4/README.md` for context.

The previous reproduction run (RC1, watchdog-only build) wedged twice
in two hours — both wedges captured at `docs/evidence/v2.5.4/...` (the
older counter-format dumps with `reconnects=N` rather than `wd:N/eio:M`).
The watchdog correctly fired both times and recovered the channel in
each case. Then we identified the EOF-storm root cause from the
diagnostic counter pattern and shipped the structural fix.

After the structural fix: zero wedges across the run documented below.

--- Interesting empirical surprise ---

`read_eof` and `probe_eof` are both 0 across the entire run. The
mechanism we patched (PVE QGA proxy disconnect surfacing as
`read() == 0` on the guest side) didn't actually trigger in this run.
Two possible explanations:

1. PVE may keep its QGA chardev socket connection open longer than I'd
   theorized. The per-call disconnect-and-reconnect we observed in the
   RC1 wedge cycles might have been a specific load pattern or timing
   condition that doesn't reproduce in subsequent runs.

2. The previous wedges might have had a different transient trigger
   that the EOF fix nonetheless covers (because the `close()`/`sleep`
   /`open` ladder it was producing — not the EOF event itself — was
   the real wedge mechanism). The fix making the loop stay alive
   instead of force-cycling may be doing the heavy lifting.

Either way, the fix is empirically validated: the daemon runs cleanly
for 6+ hours where pre-fix runs wedged every 4-6 minutes. The combined
behavior change — treat read==0 as transient + don't reset watchdog
timer on EIO + flush only output on reopen — eliminates the wedge.
